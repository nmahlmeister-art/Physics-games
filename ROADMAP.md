# Roadmap

Working notes on where these games are heading. Items marked (idea) are untested hypotheses, not commitments.

## Goal

Revision games for IGCSE Physics (Edexcel 4PH1) aimed at pupils who do not revise on their own, especially those working at grades U to 3 who are trying to reach a pass. There are plenty of tools for pupils who already want to revise. The gap is the ones who do not.

## Principles

- **Reach the pupils who will not opt in.** The teacher puts the game in front of them (lesson starter, set link). No login, works on a phone from a link.
- **Early wins.** Start easy, adapt up. Low confidence is often the real barrier.
- **A reason to come back.** Missed questions return later, day streaks, and eventually a class element.
- **Real practice, not just recognition.** Multiple choice mostly tests recognising the answer. Add question types that need the pupil to produce or work something out.
- **Learning checks are part of the game,** not a pop-up quiz. Progress should need the concept.
- **Aim at the marks low-attaining pupils can win:** recall of key facts, units and symbols, choosing and using equations, simple calculations, reading graphs.

## Where we are

- Wave Rider: Waves, with topic choice (Wave properties, The EM spectrum, Light and sound, Mixed), adaptive difficulty, come-back questions, day streak, Quit button, and a Scale lab where the pupil fits a wave to labelled axes.
- Question types in Wave Rider: multiple choice, typed numbers, fill in the blank (with spelling slack), tap-to-label diagrams (wave, compressions, reflection, EM spectrum) and sort into two groups.
- Published at the site home page, one folder per game.

## Next

1. **Test with real pupils.** A few who do not revise, in lesson time. Measure with a short topic test before and after about a week of use. This decides what else is worth building.
2. **Teacher review of every question** against the spec and exam wording before pupils rely on them.
3. **More questions, more topics.** Prioritise topics by exam weight and by where pupils struggle. Eight topics in total.

## Next round of question types

Four more types, chosen for pupils aiming for a pass. Not built yet.

1. **Put in order:** drag or tap items into order, for example the EM spectrum from lowest to highest frequency, or the steps of a calculation.
2. **Spot the mistake:** a worked answer with an error in it, such as forgetting to convert MHz to Hz, and the pupil finds it.
3. **Read the trace:** the reverse of the Scale lab. Show a wave on labelled axes and ask for the amplitude, period or frequency.
4. **Build a two-mark answer:** choose the right key words and put them in a sentence about echoes or sound in a vacuum, as a stepping stone to written answers.

Also worth adding to the existing types: typed answers with a choice of units, more tap-to-label diagrams (ray diagrams for refraction, an oscilloscope trace), and more sort sets.

## Then

- **AI used while building:** write more questions with misconception-based distractors and worked solutions; short-answer questions marked against mark-scheme key points (runs in the page, no live AI).
- **Class view (idea):** class code and a teacher view of who is playing and which questions trip people up. Needs a server and decisions about pupil data in school.
- **Installable on the home screen and playable offline.**
- **Accessibility and older phones:** contrast, text size, performance.
- **Better progress storage.** Today progress is saved per device, so shared devices mix pupils.

## Longer term (idea)

- **Live AI marking of free-text answers.** Needs a small server, running cost, a protected key, safeguarding decisions, and checking against real mark schemes. Wrong feedback harms low achievers most.
- **Motion lab:** drive a car along a road with a live distance-time or speed-time graph and questions tied to what just happened. First small test of learning inside a simulation.
- **Hub world:** a place that links the games and labs, with a timetable (a Bully-style school day). Each lesson is a short challenge, and finishing it unlocks parts of the world.
- **A 3D world** (learn motion by driving, collisions by being in one), built only if the smaller labs show that pupils link what they see to the physics.

## Learning checks inside the world

- Gates that need the concept (work out the speed to clear a gap).
- Predict, then watch.
- Measure it yourself with markers and a stopwatch, then compare with the dashboard.
- Questions at the moment of experience.
- Return to the idea days later.
- Vary the numbers and ask for working, so luck does not pass.

## Open questions

- Which kind of non-revising pupil does this reach (low confidence, sees no point, distracted)?
- Does the teacher being the author raise engagement, and for how long?
- Does playing in a simulation transfer to exam questions, or do pupils just play?
- What address should the site have long term (own domain, organisation, or current)?
